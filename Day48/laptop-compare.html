📋 How this was researched

▾

Every figure in the table above was pulled from a named, publicly available source — manufacturer spec sheets or independent lab reviews — current as of each outlet's most recent testing (2024–2026 review cycles). No numbers were invented. Where outlets disagreed, here's how conflicts were resolved:

Battery life is the messiest metric. Manufacturers quote best-case marketing numbers (Apple's "18 hrs," Dell's "27 hrs") based on video-playback loops at low brightness. Independent labs (Notebookcheck, PCWorld, Digital Trends) run mixed workloads — often web browsing plus load — and get lower, more realistic numbers. Where possible, this table uses independent lab test results over manufacturer marketing claims, and flags anything still resting on a manufacturer figure.
Performance benchmarks vary by test config. Geekbench 6 multi-core scores shift with RAM, cooling, and OS build across review units. This table uses each laptop's most commonly-cited Geekbench 6 multi-core figure from a lab review or the public Geekbench Browser database, on the mid-tier configuration each brand actually ships in volume.
Weight and price reflect base/entry configurations as sold at each brand's launch price (or the lowest widely-available current configuration), since that's what a budget-conscious buyer is most likely to actually purchase. Configurations with touchscreens, OLED panels, or discrete GPUs weigh more and cost more than the base numbers shown here.

Note on the ASUS ZenBook 14: "ZenBook 14" spans several generations and chip options across 2024–2026 (Meteor Lake, Arrow Lake, Snapdragon). This comparison uses the 2024 Meteor Lake Core Ultra 7 OLED configuration reviewed in full by PCWorld, since it's the version with a complete, independently lab-tested benchmark suite (price, weight, and Geekbench 6 all measured by one reviewer on one unit).
