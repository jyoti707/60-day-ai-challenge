The Roast

Honest, section-by-section. Here's what a recruiter thinks in the first 3 seconds.

Headline

4/10
"B.Tech IT Student | AI Web Developer | Aspiring Software Developer | DSA | Python | Full Stack Development"
Recruiter's 3-sec read: "Another student headline. Next."

❌ The problem: Six labels crammed together with no hierarchy — "AI Web Developer," "Aspiring Software Developer," and "Full Stack Development" are all fighting for attention, and none of them win. "Aspiring" is doing real damage here.

🧠 Why it hurts: Recruiters skim headlines in under 2 seconds while scrolling search results. A pile of keywords with no proof or focus reads as "hasn't figured out their positioning yet" — even if you have.

🔍 Invisible cost: "Aspiring" actively lowers your rank in some recruiter search filters, which favor confident, role-specific language. You're filtering yourself out of searches you'd otherwise appear in.

About — First 2 Lines

3/10
"I am a passionate B.Tech IT student at ABES Engineering College with a strong interest in Full Stack Web Development and Data Structures & Algorithms."
Recruiter's 3-sec read: never clicks "see more."

❌ The problem: This is the most valuable real estate on your entire profile — the only text visible before "see more" — and it opens with "I am a passionate student," the single most common opening line on LinkedIn.

🧠 Why it hurts: "Passionate" is a filler word recruiters have learned to skip past. It signals enthusiasm with zero evidence, so it reads as generic rather than credible.

🔍 Invisible cost: Most recruiters decide whether to expand a profile's About section within these two lines. You're losing the click before you get the chance to show them the internship results further down.

About — Full Section

5/10
Recruiter's read: "Fine, but forgettable."

❌ The problem: Real substance is in here (LeetCode/HackerRank practice, frontend + full-stack projects) but it's described in vague terms — "hands-on experience," "real-world projects," "efficient logic" — with zero numbers and no CTA at the end.

🧠 Why it hurts: You have hard numbers sitting one section down in your Experience (25%, 30%, 40%, 15%) that never make it into your About. Recruiters who don't scroll further never see your best proof.

🔍 Invisible cost: No CTA means even an impressed reader has no obvious next step — no "message me," no mention of internship availability. You're leaving interested recruiters with nothing to do.

Experience

5/10
Recruiter's read: "Good numbers, but I have to work to find them."

❌ The problem: This is genuinely your strongest content — 25% latency reduction, 30% query efficiency, 40% navigation improvement, 15 hrs/week saved — but it's one unbroken paragraph, not bullets. It reads like a job description, not an achievement list.

🧠 Why it hurts: Recruiters scan, they don't read paragraphs. A wall of text with strong numbers buried in the middle performs worse than short bullets with weaker numbers up front.

🔍 Invisible cost: You're getting zero credit for your best metric (15 hrs/week saved) because it's the very last clause of a 90-word sentence. Almost nobody reads that far.

Skills & Keywords

4/10
Recruiter's search filter: partially missing you.

🧠 Why it hurts: LinkedIn's search ranking weighs skill relevance to your headline and role. Non-technical, unrelated skills dilute your topical relevance for "Software Developer Intern" searches.

Day 3 — Send 10 strategic connection requests

Target: SDE recruiters and engineering managers at 5-6 mid-size product companies, plus 2nd-year+ SDE interns/new grads at companies you're targeting (they reply more often and can refer you), and alumni from ABES Engineering College now working as developers — alumni accept at far higher rates than cold recruiter requests.

Hi [Name], I'm a B.Tech IT student building full-stack projects in MERN and prepping for SDE internships. I really admire the work your team does at [Company] — would love to stay connected and learn from your journey. ≈ 210 characters

Day 4 — Comment on 5 posts in your target space

THE VALUE COMMENT FORMULA → Agree + Add Insight + Ask a Question

Agree: Open by genuinely engaging with their point — not "Great post!" but what specifically resonated.
Add Insight: Bring something from your own experience — even a small MERN/DSA lesson counts.
Ask a Question: End with a real question. Questions are what get the poster to reply, and replies are what get YOU seen by their audience.

Example: "Completely agree that latency is the silent killer of conversion — I saw a similar 25% improvement just from restructuring our REST endpoints on a recent MERN project. Curious how you'd approach caching on top of that?"

Day 5 — Publish Post #2: lessons-learned / hot take

Unpopular opinion: as a student, your GitHub commit history matters more than your GPA for landing an SDE internship. I spent the last 2 months building a MERN-stack food delivery app — real APIs, real database schema decisions, real bugs at 1am. Cutting frontend-backend latency by 25% taught me more about how the web actually works than any single course did. DSA gets you through the interview. Projects get you the interview. If you're a student reading this: ship something end-to-end before you're "ready." You'll learn faster from the parts that break. What's a project that taught you more than a class did? ≈ 640 characters

Day 6 — Engage + expand

  • Reply to every comment on both posts within a few hours — replies re-trigger the algorithm and boost reach
  • Send 5 more connection requests (repeat Day 3's targeting: recruiters, alumni, interns)
  • DM 2 people who commented meaningfully on your posts — turn engagement into an actual conversation

Day 7 — Review the numbers

Check: profile views (weekly, in your Dashboard) and connection acceptance rate (accepted ÷ sent).

  • Profile views up sharply vs. last week → your headline/keywords are working, keep posting
  • Views flat but connections accepting well → your outreach targeting is good, but your headline needs another pass
  • Acceptance rate below ~30% → your connection message is too generic or you're targeting too senior — personalize more, or target 1-2 levels closer to your own stage
  • Post views low → hook (first line) isn't stopping the scroll — rewrite the first sentence, not the whole post
